By Daniel Nthengu

Good morning, brothers and sisters.

First of all, I would like to express my gratitude to those who spoke before me. They reminded us of the importance of gratitude and of the gospel of Jesus Christ. Their words set a spiritual foundation for what I would like to share today, because gratitude naturally leads us to reflection, and reflection helps us see where the Lord is guiding our lives.

Responsibilities and Choices

As we reflect, we notice how much responsibility we carry. Each of us has expectations—as brothers and sisters, as parents and children, as church members and leaders, and as participants in our communities. We also have responsibilities at school and work. At times, these responsibilities can feel heavy, and we may wonder how we can do everything that is expected of us.

Yet, even with all these responsibilities, the Lord has given us a precious gift: the power to choose. Every day, we choose how to use our time, energy, and resources. These choices may seem small, but over time, they shape our character and determine our direction.

President Thomas S. Monson taught: “Decisions determine destiny.” This is why moments of reflection—like the beginning of a new year—are so important.

A Personal Example: Dreams and the New Year

This idea became very real to me just this week during a birthday celebration at work. We were asked to share our goals. Some said they wanted a car. Others said they wanted to fly on a plane, visit Cape Town, or get married. As I listened, I realized that behind every answer was a dream—a hope for the future.

As I thought about their dreams, my thoughts turned to the New Year. A new year invites us to pause and ask ourselves important questions:

  • What do I hope for?
  • Where am I going?
  • Am I moving closer to the person God wants me to become?

Dreams, Goals, and Resolutions

To understand this better, it helps to define the relationship between dreams, goals, and resolutions:

Dreams – the desires God places in our hearts to help us grow.

Goals – the steps we take to act on those dreams.

Resolutions – the commitments we make to stay faithful to those goals.

The Lord teaches us that progress usually comes in small and steady ways: “By small and simple things are great things brought to pass.” (Alma 37:6) This principle gives us hope when change feels slow or difficult.

The Gospel and the Doctrine of Christ

As members of the Church, we are blessed to know that our dreams and goals are not meant to exist in isolation. They are to be guided by the gospel of Jesus Christ, which gives us both direction and purpose.

The gospel teaches that we access the saving and redeeming power of Jesus Christ through:

  • Faith in Him
  • Repentance
  • Baptism
  • Receiving the gift of the Holy Ghost
  • Enduring to the end (3 Nephi 27)

This is known as the doctrine of Christ. The Book of Mormon teaches this doctrine clearly in 2 Nephi 31, 2 Nephi 32, and 3 Nephi 11. When we live this doctrine, we come unto Christ and are saved.

When our dreams, goals, and resolutions are centered on the doctrine of Christ, they become more than personal ambitions—they become part of our discipleship.

Hyrum Smith taught: “Preach the first principles of the Gospel—preach them over again.” As we return often to these simple truths, the Lord reveals greater understanding and strengthens our resolve.

Staying Focused on Our Goals and on the Savior

To remain faithful to our goals and dreams, we must stay centered on the Savior. This requires daily, intentional practices:

Daily Prayer: Prayer aligns our hearts with God’s will. It helps us seek guidance, strength, and clarity. Through sincere prayer, the Spirit helps us recognize what truly matters and where we may need to change.

Consistent Scripture Study: Studying God’s word daily anchors us in truth. Even a few minutes each day provides direction, comfort, and motivation to stay faithful.

Christ-Centered Goals: Goals that emphasize service, faith, and character development help us focus on becoming more like the Savior, rather than just achieving personal success.

Small, Consistent Actions: Great things come from small beginnings. One faithful step at a time allows the Lord to magnify our efforts and helps us progress steadily without becoming overwhelmed.

Self-Reflection and Repentance: Repentance allows us to course-correct when we fall short. It is a sign of faith, not failure. Through Christ’s Atonement, we are renewed and strengthened to continue forward with hope.

Remembering the Savior through the Sacrament: Partaking of the sacrament each week helps us renew our commitment to Him. It reminds us of His sacrifice and love and recommits us to follow Him daily.

Companionship of the Holy Ghost: The Spirit guides, warns, and comforts us. It helps us remain spiritually sensitive and focused as we live the gospel and pursue righteous goals.

Guidance from Prophets and Scripture

“Nothing invites the Spirit more than fixing your focus on Jesus Christ.” —President Russell M. Nelson

“When we put God first, all other things fall into their proper place or drop out of our lives.” —President Ezra Taft Benson

“Great things are accomplished by small, consistent efforts over time.” —President Dieter F. Uchtdorf

“Decisions determine destiny.” —President Thomas S. Monson

“Look unto me in every thought; doubt not, fear not.” —Doctrine and Covenants 6:36

Conclusion

The New Year is not just a change on the calendar—it is an invitation from the Lord to grow. Step by step, small effort by small effort, Jesus Christ helps us become who He knows we can be.

I testify that Jesus Christ lives. I know that His gospel is true. And I know that as we align our dreams, goals, and resolutions with His doctrine, He will guide our path and fill our lives with purpose, hope, and joy.

In the name of Jesus Christ, amen.
